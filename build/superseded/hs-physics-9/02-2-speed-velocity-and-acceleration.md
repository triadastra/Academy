# 2.2 Speed, Velocity, and Acceleration

This lesson defines the three quantities that describe how motion changes, and distinguishes the average value of each from its instantaneous value. The recurring theme is that speed and velocity are not synonyms: one is a scalar and one is a vector, and problems are designed to punish treating them as interchangeable.

## Speed

**Speed** is the distance an object travels per unit of time:

$$v = \frac{d}{t}$$

**Average speed** is used when speed is not constant:

$$\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$$

When speed is constant, the average speed is that constant value.

**Instantaneous speed** is the speed at a particular moment.

## Velocity

**Velocity** is speed in a given direction. It can be shown with an arrow, and for motion in a straight line, direction is represented with a **+ or − sign**.

**Instantaneous velocity** is the velocity at a particular instant of time. **If velocity is constant, average velocity equals instantaneous velocity.**

Average velocity is proportional to **displacement**, not distance — which produces the key consequence:

> **Objects with the same average speed can have different velocities.**

Two runners covering the same track in the same time have equal average speed; if one finishes where they started, their average velocity is zero and the other's is not.

## Acceleration

**Acceleration** is the rate at which an object's velocity changes:

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - v_i}{t_f - t_i}$$

Its units are $\text{m/s}^2$, though other combinations such as $\text{m/s per hour}$ or $\text{km/h}^2$ are valid.

**Instantaneous acceleration** is the change in velocity at a specific moment.

Two facts follow directly from the definition:

- **Acceleration is zero when velocity is constant.**
- If velocity changes at a constant rate, **acceleration is constant** — this is **uniformly accelerated motion**.

**Acceleration points in the direction of the velocity change**, not the direction of motion. An object slowing down while moving in the positive direction has negative acceleration, and this is the single most common source of sign errors.

## Kinds of motion

- **Uniform motion** — the object moves in a straight line with constant velocity.
- **Nonuniform motion** — velocity changes along a straight line.
- Motion may also be **not confined to a straight line**, which is the subject of two-dimensional motion.

On a motion diagram, average acceleration vectors show how velocity is changing between successive intervals.
