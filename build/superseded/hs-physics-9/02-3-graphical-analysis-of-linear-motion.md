# 2.3 Graphical Analysis of Linear Motion

Graphs of motion carry the same information as the equations, but make it visible. This lesson covers the three graph types and what to extract from each. Two rules do most of the work: **the slope of a graph gives the rate of change**, and **the area under a graph gives the accumulated quantity**.

## Position–time graphs

Time goes on the horizontal axis and position on the vertical axis. The $y$-axis value represents the **instantaneous position**, and the $y$-intercept is the **initial position**.

**The slope indicates the average velocity**:

$$\text{slope} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{\text{displacement}}{\text{time interval}}$$

The unit of the slope is metres per second. A **steeper slope means a greater change in position**, and therefore a larger $|v|$.

Reading the shape:

- **No motion (at rest)** — a horizontal straight line, parallel to the $x$-axis.
- **Uniform motion** — a slanted straight line. With constant velocity, instantaneous velocity equals average velocity.
- **Accelerated motion** — either several straight lines of different slopes, meaning several different velocities, or a curve.

On a curve, the two velocities are found differently: **average velocity is the slope of the line connecting two points** on the curve, while **instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent** at a single point.

An **intersection of two lines indicates that the two objects meet** — same position at the same time.

A **best-fit line** shows likely positions between data points.

Note that a **distance–time graph never has negative values**, because distance is a scalar. A position–time graph can go negative; a distance–time graph cannot.

## Velocity–time graphs

The $y$-axis shows the velocity of the object at any moment.

Two quantities can be extracted:

- **The slope is the acceleration.**
- **The area under the graph is the displacement.**

Reading the shape:

- **Uniform motion ($a = 0$)** — a horizontal line: velocity constant, acceleration zero.
- **Uniformly accelerated motion** — a slanted straight line, whose slope is the acceleration. Since $a$ is constant, instantaneous acceleration equals average acceleration. A **rising line means $a > 0$**, a **descending line means $a < 0$**, and a steeper slope means a larger $|a|$.
- **Motion with changing acceleration** — a curve, where instantaneous acceleration is the slope of the tangent at that moment.

Signs matter when reading area: **area above the $t$-axis is positive displacement, area below is negative**. Area below the $v = 0$ line means the object moved backwards. Two objects **meet when the areas under their graphs are equal**.

As with speed, a **speed–time graph has no negative values**.

## Acceleration–time graphs

- **At rest or in uniform motion** — $a = 0$, a horizontal line along the $t$-axis.
- **Uniformly accelerated motion** — a horizontal line parallel to the $t$-axis at the constant value of $a$.

## The pattern

The three graphs form a chain. Differentiating — taking the slope — moves down it: position → velocity → acceleration. Integrating — taking the area — moves back up: acceleration → velocity → displacement. Knowing which operation applies to which graph is most of graphical analysis.
