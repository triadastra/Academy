# 6.2 Civilizations of Mesoamerica

## The Olmec (1400–300 BCE)

The **first civilisation in Mesoamerica**, who called themselves the **"Xi"** people. They are known as the **"Mother Culture"** because their influence spread to the cultures that followed.

The Olmec were the **first to use stone in art and architecture**, and their temples and art indicate **powerful priests and nobles** — job specialisation and social class, in the terms of 1.1.

Their achievements included complex **irrigation and urban piping**, **metallurgy**, and **astronomy** so central that cities were arranged by the stars. **Trade with neighbours** produced **cultural diffusion** — the jaguar imagery that recurs across Mesoamerica begins here.

**Religion:** jaguars represented rulers; snakes, crocodiles, and sharks also featured. **Shamans and healers assumed animal powers.**

**Decline:** there are **few decipherable written records**, so nobody really knows how the Olmec declined. This is worth noting as a genuine limit of the evidence rather than a gap to be filled with speculation.

## The Zapotecs (500 BCE – 1000 CE)

The Zapotecs had **similar traditions to the Olmecs**: a small ruling class, aggressive conquest, **human sacrifice**, **hieroglyphics that recorded conquests**, and **ball games**.

They were the **first great stone pyramid builders**, centred on **Monte Albán**.

## The Maya

The Maya were organised as **powerful city-states**. They were **never an empire and never politically united**, but traded regularly with one another — honey, salt, cotton, and cacao; feathers, jade, and jaguar pelts.

**Agriculture and storage:** **chultunes** were underground granaries.

**Religion:** they worshipped **hundreds of gods**, with overlapping duties, and practised **sacrifice** of people captured in battle.

### Maya achievements

| Field | Achievement |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | Pyramids, palaces, and **observatories aligned with the stars** |
| **Writing** | **Hieroglyphic** writing; **codices** — books made of bark paper |
| **Calendar** | A **365-day solar calendar**; tracked planets and eclipses |
| **Mathematics** | Invented the concept of **zero**; an advanced mathematical system |
| **Medicine** | Almost entirely **herbal** |

The independent invention of **zero** is the standout. It was developed separately in India (2.1) and in Mesoamerica, with no contact between them — an example of two civilisations arriving at the same profound abstraction because both needed positional notation for astronomical calculation.

**Decline:** the Maya city-states collapsed over several centuries, with **climate change — particularly drought — a contributing factor**.

## The Aztecs

The Aztecs (Mexica) spoke **Nahuatl**. They **founded Tenochtitlán in 1325** and **conquered the surrounding city-states in 1434**.

**Founding of Tenochtitlán.** Aztec legend places the founding on an island in **Lake Texcoco**, a swampy lake — unpromising ground that the Aztecs engineered into one of the largest cities in the world.

**The Triple Alliance** united the Mexica of **Tenochtitlán** with **Texcoco** and **Tlacopan**, ruling a population of **5–6 million**. **Tribute** from subject peoples is what allowed them to build up Tenochtitlán.

**Flower Wars** were intermittent ritual wars between the Triple Alliance and subject or rival city-states, fought for years:

- Combatants met at **predetermined sites with equal numbers** of forces
- **Small-scale battles with melee weapons**, to display individual skill

The purpose was not territorial conquest but the **capture of prisoners** — which connects directly to the religion below.

**Religion.** The Aztecs were **polytheistic**. **Huitzilopochtli**, deity of war and the sun, was the patron god of the Aztecs and of Tenochtitlán. **Nanahuatzin** had sacrificed himself to become the sun. **Quetzalcoatl** was the feathered serpent god.

**Sacrifices were made to give strength to the sun**, and the victims were usually **prisoners of war**. The theology and the warfare form a closed system: the sun requires blood, war supplies captives, and the Flower Wars guarantee a supply without the cost of full-scale conquest.

Their **calendar was inspired by the Maya**.

### Achievements

| Field | Achievement |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | **Tenochtitlán** with **causeways, canals, and chinampas** (floating gardens) |
| **Writing** | **Pictograms** in codices |
| **Calendar** | A 365-day calendar plus a **260-day ritual calendar** |
| **Mathematics** | Used for land measurement and trade |

**Chinampas** deserve emphasis: artificial islands of mud and vegetation built up in the lake, producing several harvests a year. They are the reason a city on a swamp could feed a quarter of a million people.

## Art and culture across Mesoamerica

Pottery, weaving, and jewellery; **music and dance used in ceremonies**; religion and mythology depicted in sculpture and murals.
