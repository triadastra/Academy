# 1.1 The Beginning of Civilization

Before there were states, laws, or writing, there were people who moved with their food. Everything else in this course follows from what happened when they stopped moving.

## The Stone Ages

**Old Stone Age (Palaeolithic), 2.4 million BCE – c. 10,000 BCE**

- Learned to use **simple tools and fire**
- **Nomadic hunter-gatherers**
- Started using **language**

*Society:* labour was divided by gender — men hunted, fought, and did heavy labour; women gathered, cooked, and raised children. Groups were **tribal**, based on extended families, with small populations.

*Artistic expression:* cave paintings, most famously at **Lascaux, France (c. 15,000 BCE)**.

*Religious belief:* burying the dead, and **animism** — the belief in a world full of spirits.

**Middle Stone Age (Mesolithic)** — the transition between the Old and New Stone Ages.

**New Stone Age (Neolithic), c. 10,000 – 3,000 BCE** — the **Agricultural Revolution**, and the appearance of **river valley civilisations**.

**Bronze Age (from c. 4,000 BCE)** — metalworking began with copper, then a **bronze** alloy of copper and tin. This developed **independently** in several places, including China and Mesopotamia — which is itself an important point: similar solutions arose separately because similar problems did.

## The Agricultural Revolution

Farming is the hinge of the whole story. A hunter-gatherer band must move to where the food is; a farming village stays where the food grows. Staying still makes possible everything a nomadic band cannot have: stored surplus, permanent buildings, specialised crafts, accumulated property — and therefore inequality, government, and record-keeping.

## The first civilisations, c. 3,500 BCE

Four river valleys:

| River | Modern region |
|---|---|
| **Tigris and Euphrates** | Iraq |
| **Nile** | Egypt |
| **Indus** | Pakistan and India |
| **Huanghe (Yellow River)** | China |

All four are river valleys, and for the same reason: rivers supply water for irrigation, deposit fertile silt when they flood, and provide routes for trade and travel.

## The eight features of a civilisation

1. **Cities**
2. **Organised government**
3. **Complex religions**
4. **Job specialisation**
5. **Social classes**
6. **Arts and architecture**
7. **Public works**
8. **Writing**

Use this list as a checklist throughout the course. Whenever a new society appears, ask which of the eight it has — and where a society is missing one, ask why. The Inca, for example, built roads, cities, and a vast state without a **writing** system (see 5.4), which is exactly the sort of exception worth explaining rather than ignoring.

Note also that **job specialisation** is the feature that makes most of the others possible. Only a farming surplus frees some people from producing food, and only then can there be full-time priests, soldiers, scribes, and artisans — which is to say, social classes, organised government, and writing.
