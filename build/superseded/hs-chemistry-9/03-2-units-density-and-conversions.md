# 3.2 Units, Density, and Conversion Problems

This lesson covers the SI units and prefixes, the derived quantities used constantly in chemistry, and dimensional analysis — the technique that turns a unit conversion into an algebra problem.

## The seven base SI units

All metric units are based on **multiples of 10**.

| Quantity | Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Length | metre | m |
| Time | second | s |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

## Common prefixes

| Prefix | Symbol | Factor |

| Mega | M | $10^{6}$ |
| Kilo | k | $10^{3}$ |

| Centi | c | $10^{-2}$ |

## Volume

$$1\ \text{L} = 1\ \text{dm}^3 = 1000\ \text{mL}, \qquad 1\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$$

The equivalence $1\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$ is used constantly and is worth memorising rather than deriving each time.

## Mass

One **gram** is the mass of $1\ \text{cm}^3$ of water at 4 °C — the definition from which the metric system's convenient water relationships follow.

## Energy

**Energy** is the capacity to do work or produce heat. The SI unit is the **joule (J)**; the common non-SI unit is the **calorie**, with $1\ \text{J} = 0.2390\ \text{cal}$.

## Temperature

**Temperature** measures how hot or cold an object is.

$$K = °C + 273, \qquad °C = K - 273$$

More precisely the offset is **273.15**. **Absolute zero**, $0\ \text{K}$, equals $-273.15\ °C$ — the lowest possible temperature.

Note that a temperature *difference* is the same number in kelvin and in Celsius, since the two scales differ only by an offset.

## Density

**Density** is the ratio of an object's mass to its volume:

$$\text{Density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$$

The SI unit is $\text{kg/m}^3$, though $\text{g/cm}^3$ is more common in the laboratory.

Density is an **intensive** property: it does not depend on how much of the substance you have, which is why it can be used to identify a substance.

## Conversion factors and dimensional analysis

A **conversion factor** is a **ratio of equivalent measurements**, and therefore equals 1.

The pattern to remember: the **smaller unit is associated with the larger number**, and the larger unit with the smaller number. In $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$, the centimetre is smaller and carries the larger figure.

Conversion factors are **defined or exact quantities**, so they have an **unlimited number of significant figures** and never limit the precision of a result.

**Dimensional analysis** solves problems using the units of the measurements. Arrange each conversion factor so the unwanted unit cancels, exactly as in Physics 1.1. To express 750 nm in metres:

$$750\ \text{nm} \times \frac{10^{-9}\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{nm}} = 7.50 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$$

Writing the units out and checking that they cancel is the reliable way to confirm the factor is the right way up — a wrong-way-up factor gives an answer off by the square of the conversion.
