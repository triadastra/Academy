# 2.2 Writing Literary Analysis

Good analysis identifies devices. Excellent analysis turns those observations into an argument about the text. This lesson covers the paragraph structure that gets you to competent, and the five moves that get you past it.

## PEEL: the paragraph structure

**1. Point (P)** — state the claim of the paragraph, connected to your thesis.

**2. Evidence (E)** — introduce and quote from the text. Keep the quotation short and place it in context.

**3. Explanation (E)** — analyse the evidence. **Explain how the evidence supports your point — don't assume the reader will make the connection.** Discuss techniques, themes, or effects: literary devices, historical context, cause and effect.

**4. Link (L)** — connect back to the thesis. Reinforce how this point contributes to the overall argument, and transition smoothly to the next paragraph.

The Explanation step is where nearly all lost marks live. A paragraph that quotes accurately and then moves on has shown that you found the line, not that you understood it.

## Five ways to write excellent analysis

### 1. Turn your analysis into argument

Insightful analysis is not just close reading — it is a **form of argument**. Instead of merely observing devices, frame your analysis as a claim about human behaviour, ideology, or emotional truth.

> **Weaker:** O'Brien uses metafiction to show how storytelling helps him process trauma.
>
> **Stronger:** O'Brien's metafictional layers suggest that the line between fiction and truth dissolves under trauma — not because memory fails, but because reality itself becomes unbearable to report plainly.

The difference: one **identifies a device**, the other **takes a stand**.

### 2. Layer interpretations, not just evidence

Go beyond "what this means" to "what this **also** could mean". Look for **interpretive friction** — places where one reading pulls against another. **Tension is where the complexity lives.**

Mary Anne's transformation into a feral killer is not only a loss of innocence. It is also a metaphor for war's erotic pull, for the colonial fantasy of Vietnam, and for the collapse of Western femininity under foreign pressure.

Ask: *what's beneath the obvious?*

### 3. Collapse the distance between form and meaning

Don't treat literary devices as separate from content. Make your analysis show that **the form is the content**.

> **Weaker:** The repetition emphasises the speaker's trauma.
>
> **Stronger:** The obsessive repetition **enacts** trauma — forcing the reader into the same loop of memory the speaker cannot escape.

This is more than pointing out a technique. It is showing that **how something is written is what it means**.

### 4. Make the text uncomfortable

Excellent analysis is unafraid to **point out contradictions in theme or tone**, and to **challenge what the author might be avoiding**.

O'Brien presents storytelling as healing, but his recursive grief over Kiowa and the Vietnamese soldier suggests it may equally be a way of never finishing the mourning.

This shows that you are not just following the text but **wrestling with it** — and examiners can tell the difference immediately.

### 5. Elevate with thematic synthesis

The highest-level writing does not just explain parts of a text; it **braids themes together into new insight**.

Take "masculinity" and "memory" in *The Things They Carried*. The men's silence around fear — framed as masculinity — is not only emotional repression; it is an act of **editing memory**. Their manhood depends on which version of the past they agree to tell. In O'Brien's world, masculinity is not about truth but about the stories men allow each other to believe.

That is **synthesis** — not merely linking two themes, but fusing them into a deeper argument about identity, narrative, and social structure.

## Writing comparisons

When comparing two texts, introduce and quote from both, and organise by **point of comparison** rather than by text. A paragraph that summarises text A and then summarises text B is two summaries, not a comparison; a paragraph that examines how each text handles the same problem is an argument.
