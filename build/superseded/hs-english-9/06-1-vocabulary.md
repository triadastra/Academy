# 6.1 Vocabulary

The vocabulary section rewards two things: knowing the definition, and being able to **use the word in a sentence that demonstrates the definition**. A sentence that would work equally well with the wrong word earns nothing.

| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permeate** | Verb | To spread through something | The smell of garlic permeated the entire house. |
| **Pilgrimage** | Noun | A journey to a sacred place for religious reasons | The Muslims made a pilgrimage to Mecca. |
| **Putrid** | Adjective | Decaying and foul-smelling | The putrid smell made them leave the cellar immediately. |
| **Redeem** | Verb | To make up for faults or bad behaviour | He tried to redeem himself with good deeds. |

## Studying vocabulary effectively

Learn each word with a sentence you wrote yourself, not only the one supplied. Two words in this list are especially easy to confuse in use:

- **Gargantuan** and **gigantic** are near-synonyms, but *gargantuan* carries a hint of the grotesque or excessive.
- **Genuine** and **legitimate** both mean *real*, but *legitimate* implies conforming to a rule or law, while *genuine* implies not being a forgery or a pretence.

Where two words are close, the mark is usually for choosing the more precise one.
