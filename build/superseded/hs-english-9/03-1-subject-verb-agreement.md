# 3.1 Subject–Verb Agreement

Every question in this section reduces to one operation: **find the real subject, decide whether it is singular or plural, then match the verb.** Most errors come from the first step, not the third.

## The basic rule

- **Singular subject → singular verb** (*is, was, has, likes*).
- **Plural subject → plural verb** (*are, were, have, like*).

> The cat **is** sleeping. (singular)
> My friends **like** soccer. (plural)

## Indefinite pronouns

**Always singular:** *everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, no one, nobody, anyone, each, either, neither*.

> Each student **has** a textbook.

**Always plural:** *both, few, many, several*.

**Singular or plural depending on context:** *all, some, none, any*. When referring to a **countable** noun they take a plural verb; when referring to an **uncountable** quantity, singular.

## Compound subjects

**Joined by "and"** — usually plural.

> Mary and Sue **have** a nice room.

**Joined by "or" / "nor"** — the verb agrees with the **closest** noun.

> Either the books or the notebook **belongs** to me. *(notebook = singular)*
> Neither she nor her brothers **enjoy** hiking. *(brothers = plural)*

Notice that reversing the order of the two nouns changes the correct verb. That is the whole rule.

## Collective nouns

*Family, team, class, committee, jury* can be singular or plural:

- **Acting as one unit → singular.** The team **is** winning.
- **Members doing their own thing → plural** (more common in British English). The team **are** fighting.

> The jury **debates** the verdict.

## Ignore prepositional phrases

The verb matches the **main subject**, not the words in between.

> The list of items **is** on the table.

"List" is the subject, not "items". Crossing out the prepositional phrase before choosing the verb solves nearly every question of this type.

## Tricky singulars

**Uncountable nouns** — *news, money, physics*:

> The news **was** bad.
> Ten dollars **is** too much for a snack. *(amounts = singular)*

**Academic subjects and titles** — singular even when they end in *-s*:

> Physics **requires** strong maths skills.
> *The Lord of the Rings* **is** my favourite book.

## Data and media: formal versus informal

*Data* is technically plural (*datum* is the singular), but is often treated as singular in informal usage.

- **Formal:** The data **suggest** a problem.
- **Informal:** The data **shows** a problem.

If the question is marked as formal writing, choose the plural.

## Practice

Choose the correct verb:

1. The cat (is / are) sleeping on the couch.
2. My friends (likes / like) to play soccer.
3. Each student (has / have) a textbook.
4. The news (was / were) surprising.
5. Neither the teacher nor the students (knows / know) the answer.
6. The team (are / is) arguing about the rules.
7. Either the books or the notebook (belong / belongs) to me.
8. Ten dollars (is / are) too much for a snack.
9. Physics (require / requires) strong maths skills.
10. The jury (debate / debates) the verdict.
11. The list of items (is / are) on the table.
12. Neither she nor her brothers (enjoy / enjoys) hiking.
13. The data (suggest / suggests) a problem.
14. All the workers in our company (speak / speaks) Arabic.
15. None of the employees (speak / speaks) Spanish.

**Answers:**

1. is 2. like 3. has 4. was 5. know
6. is 7. belongs 8. is 9. requires 10. debates
11. is 12. enjoy 13. suggest *(formal)* 14. speak 15. speaks

Question 6 is worth checking against the rule: because the members are arguing with each other rather than acting as one unit, "are" is also defensible — but the answer key treats *team* as a single unit. Question 15 takes the singular because *none* here means "not one".
