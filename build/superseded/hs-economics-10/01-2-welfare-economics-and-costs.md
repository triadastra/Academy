# 1.2 Welfare Economics, Costs, and Production

## Welfare economics

**Welfare economics** is the study of how the allocation of resources affects economic well-being (which can be measured by GDP per capita).

**What determines the allocation of resources:**

- How much of each good is produced
- Which producers produce it
- Which consumers consume it

## Surplus

| Measure | Definition | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer surplus (CS)** | What a buyer is willing to pay minus what they actually pay | willingness to pay − P |
| **Producer surplus (PS)** | What a seller is paid minus the seller's cost | P − cost |
| **Total surplus (TS)** | Total gains from trade in a market | CS + PS |

Total surplus is the standard measure of efficiency: an outcome is **efficient** when it maximises total surplus, and interventions such as price controls or tariffs are usually evaluated by how much surplus they destroy.

## Consumer equilibrium

A consumer buying goods X and Y maximises utility where the **marginal utility per dollar** is equal across goods:

$$\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = \frac{MU_y}{P_y}$$

The reasoning: if a dollar spent on X yielded more utility than a dollar spent on Y, the consumer would move spending toward X — and would keep doing so until the ratios equalised, since marginal utility falls as consumption rises.

Note that the condition uses **marginal** utility divided by **price**, not total utility and not marginal utility alone. Both of the tempting wrong answers ($TU_x = TU_y$ and $MU_x = MU_y$) ignore that goods have different prices.

## Costs

**Explicit cost** — actual money paid out.

**Implicit cost** — the value of the forgone benefits: **opportunity costs and trade-offs**.

$$\text{explicit cost} + \text{implicit cost} = \text{total opportunity cost}$$

## Economic versus accounting profit

**Economic profit** = revenue − explicit cost − implicit cost

**Accounting profit** = revenue − explicit cost

**Economic profit is often less than accounting profit**, because it subtracts the implicit costs an accountant does not record.

*Worked example:* Bruce is a talented writer and graphic artist and enjoys both equally. He gives up $45,000 as a writer for a job as an artist. His accounting profit ignores the forgone $45,000; his economic profit subtracts it. If he earns less than $45,000 as an artist, he is making an economic loss even though his accountant reports income.

**Normal profit** is a **zero economic profit** — just enough to keep the resources employed in their current activity. This is why zero economic profit is not a sign of failure: it means the firm is doing exactly as well as its next-best alternative.

## The production possibility curve

The **PPC** shows the combinations of two goods an economy can produce given its capital, labour, and technology. Points on the curve are efficient, points inside are inefficient, and points outside are unattainable. The **slope** of the curve is the opportunity cost of one good in terms of the other.

## Inputs and time horizons

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Fixed input** | An input whose quantity is fixed for a period and cannot be varied |
| **Variable input** | An input whose quantity the firm can vary at any time |
| **Short run** | A period in which **at least one input is fixed** |
| **Long run** | A period in which **all inputs can be varied** |

The short run and long run are defined by **what can change**, not by any particular length of time.

| Cost | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Fixed cost** | Does **not** depend on the quantity of output produced |
| **Variable cost** | Depends on the quantity produced |

*Worked example:* if total fixed costs are $46, the average product of labour is 5 units when 10 units are produced, and the wage is $12 — then producing 10 units requires 2 units of labour, costing $24. Total cost is $46 + $24 = $70, and average total cost is $70/10 = **$7**.

## Profit and the break-even point

$$\text{If } TR > TC \Rightarrow \text{profitable};\quad TR = TC \Rightarrow \text{break even};\quad TR < TC \Rightarrow \text{loss}$$

The **break-even price** is the market price at which the firm earns **zero economic profit**. Comparing price with minimum average total cost:

| Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Market price **>** minimum ATC | Profitable |
| Market price **=** minimum ATC | Breaks even |
| Market price **<** minimum ATC | Unprofitable |

**Marginal cost.** If labour is the only variable input, costs $15 per hour, and has a marginal product of 3 units per hour, then the short-run marginal cost of one unit is $15/3 = **$5**. In general, $MC = w/MP_L$.
