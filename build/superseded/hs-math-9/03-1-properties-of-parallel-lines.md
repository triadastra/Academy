# 3.1 Properties of Parallel Lines

When a line crosses two parallel lines, the angles formed come in predictable pairs. This lesson names those pairs and states the results connecting them — the toolkit used in nearly every geometry proof that follows.

## The transversal

A **transversal** is a line that intersects two coplanar lines at **two distinct points**.

The transversal creates eight angles, and the pairs are classified by position:

- **Corresponding angles** occupy the same relative position at each intersection.
- **Alternate interior angles** lie between the two lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
- **Same-side interior angles** lie between the two lines, on the same side of the transversal.
- **Alternate exterior angles** lie outside the two lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
- **Same-side exterior angles** lie outside the two lines, on the same side of the transversal.

Two questions identify any pair: **interior or exterior** (between the lines or outside them), and **alternate or same-side** (opposite sides of the transversal or the same side).

## The results

**Postulate 3-1 (Corresponding Angles Postulate):** If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then **corresponding angles are congruent**.

This is taken as a postulate; everything else in the lesson is proved from it.

**Theorem 3-1 (Alternate Interior Angles Theorem):** If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then **alternate interior angles are congruent**.

**Theorem 3-2 (Same-Side Interior Angles Theorem):** If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then **same-side interior angles are supplementary**.

**Theorem 3-3 (Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem):** If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then **alternate exterior angles are congruent**.

**Theorem 3-4 (Same-Side Exterior Angles Theorem):** If a transversal intersects two parallel lines, then **same-side exterior angles are supplementary**.

## The pattern

Rather than memorising five separate statements, notice the rule:

> **Alternate pairs are congruent. Same-side pairs are supplementary.**

Whether the angles are interior or exterior does not change which relationship holds — it only changes where the angles are found. Same-side pairs are supplementary because one of them is the vertical angle, or the linear-pair partner, of an angle congruent to the other.

## A caution about direction

Every statement above begins "**if a transversal intersects two parallel lines**". The parallelism is the hypothesis, and the angle relationship is the conclusion. Using these theorems to *prove* lines parallel would be assuming the converse — which is the subject of the next lesson, where the converses are established separately.
