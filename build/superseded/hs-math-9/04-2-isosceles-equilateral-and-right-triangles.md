# 4.2 Isosceles, Equilateral, and Right Triangles

Special triangles have extra properties that follow from their symmetry. This lesson covers the isosceles triangle theorem and its converse, the equilateral case, and the results specific to right triangles.

## Parts of an isosceles triangle

- The **legs** are the congruent sides.
- The **base** is the third side.
- The **vertex angle** is the angle formed by the two congruent sides.
- The **base angles** are the other two.

## The Isosceles Triangle Theorem

**Theorem 4-3 (Isosceles Triangle Theorem):** If two sides of a triangle are congruent, then the **angles opposite those sides are congruent**.

In $\triangle ABC$, if $AB = AC$, then $\angle B \cong \angle C$.

**Theorem 4-4 (Converse):** If two **angles** of a triangle are congruent, then the **sides opposite** them are congruent. In $\triangle ABC$, if $\angle B \cong \angle C$, then $AB = AC$.

Because both a statement and its converse hold, the relationship is biconditional: **a triangle has two congruent sides if and only if it has two congruent angles.** Either fact may be used to deduce the other.

**Theorem 4-5:** The bisector of the vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is the **perpendicular bisector of the base**.

This single line does three jobs at once — bisects the vertex angle, bisects the base, and meets the base at right angles — which is why it is so often the auxiliary line to draw in an isosceles-triangle proof.

## Corollaries for equilateral triangles

A **corollary** is a statement that follows immediately from a theorem.

**Corollary to Theorem 4-3:** If a triangle is **equilateral**, then it is **equiangular**.

An equilateral triangle has three congruent interior angles, each therefore $60°$, since they total $180°$.

## Right triangles

The **Pythagorean theorem**: in right triangle $ABC$ with $m\angle C = 90°$,

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

where $c$ is the **hypotenuse**, the longest side.

The **converse of the Pythagorean theorem** also holds: if the side lengths satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, the triangle is right-angled — the test used in 3.3.

### The 30-60-90 triangle

In right triangle $ABC$ with $m\angle C = 90°$, if $m\angle A = 30°$, then $BC = \tfrac{1}{2}AB$, and the sides are in the ratio

$$1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$$

The side opposite the $30°$ angle is exactly half the hypotenuse. Recognising this ratio removes the need for trigonometry in many problems.

### The median to the hypotenuse

If $D$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $AB$, then $CD = \tfrac{1}{2}AB$ — the median to the hypotenuse of a right triangle is half the hypotenuse, so $D$ is equidistant from all three vertices.
