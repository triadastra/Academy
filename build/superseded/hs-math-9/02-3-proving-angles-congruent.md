# 2.3 Proving Angles Congruent

This lesson applies deductive reasoning to produce the first theorems of the course. A **theorem** differs from a postulate in that it is *proved* rather than assumed — each of the results below follows from the definitions and properties already established.

## Vertical angles

**Theorem 2-1 (Vertical Angles Theorem):** **Vertical angles are congruent.**

Vertical angles are the pairs of opposite angles formed when two lines intersect. The proof rests on the fact that each of them is supplementary to the same third angle, which is exactly what the next theorem generalises.

## Congruent supplements and complements

**Theorem 2-2 (Congruent Supplements Theorem):** If two angles are supplements of the same angle — or of congruent angles — then **the two angles are congruent**.

Supplementary angles sum to 180°. If $\angle A + \angle C = 180°$ and $\angle B + \angle C = 180°$, then $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ must be equal, since both equal $180° - \angle C$.

**Theorem 2-3 (Congruent Complements Theorem):** If two angles are complements of the same angle — or of congruent angles — then the two angles are congruent.

Complementary angles sum to 90°, and the reasoning is identical.

These two theorems have the same shape: **two angles that are related in the same way to the same third angle must be congruent to each other.** Recognising that shape is more useful than memorising the statements separately.

## Right angles

**Theorem 2-4:** **All right angles are congruent.**

Every right angle measures 90°, so any two have equal measure.

**Theorem 2-5:** If two angles are **congruent and supplementary**, then **each is a right angle**.

The proof is a short calculation: if the angles are congruent, call each $x$; if they are supplementary, $x + x = 180°$, so $2x = 180°$ and $x = 90°$.

Both conditions are required. Congruent angles need not be right angles, and supplementary angles need not be either — it is the combination that forces the result.
