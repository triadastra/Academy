# 1.3 Segments, Rays, Parallel Lines, and Planes

A line extends forever, but most figures are built from its parts. This lesson defines segments and rays, then classifies the ways two lines can be related — including the case that only exists in three dimensions.

## Parts of a line

- A **segment** is the part of a line consisting of **two endpoints and all points between** them.
- A **ray** is the part of a line consisting of **one endpoint** and all the points of the line on **one side** of that endpoint.

**Opposite rays** are two collinear rays with a common endpoint. Opposite rays **always form a line** — together they cover the whole line, extending without end in both directions from the shared endpoint.

Note the requirement that they be *collinear*. Two rays from the same endpoint that are not collinear form an angle, not a line.

## Relationships between lines

Two lines in space can be related in three ways:

- **Parallel lines** are **coplanar** lines that do not intersect.
- **Skew lines** are **noncoplanar** lines. They do not intersect and are not parallel.
- **Intersecting lines** meet at exactly one point.

The distinction between parallel and skew is the one worth care. Both fail to intersect; the difference is whether they lie in the same plane. Skew lines exist only in three dimensions — in a plane, two lines that do not intersect must be parallel.

**Parallel planes** are planes that do not intersect.

## Always, sometimes, or never

- **Two parallel lines are _____ coplanar.** *Always* — it is part of the definition.
- **Two planes that do not intersect are _____ parallel.** *Always*.
- **Two lines that lie in parallel planes are _____ parallel.** *Sometimes* — they never meet, since their planes never meet, but they may be skew rather than parallel.
- **Two lines in intersecting planes are _____ skew.** *Sometimes*.

The third of these is the instructive one. Failing to intersect is not sufficient for parallelism; the lines must also be coplanar.

## Measuring segments

**Postulate 1-5 (the Ruler Postulate)** — the points of a line can be put into one-to-one correspondence with the real numbers. This is what allows distance to be measured at all: it attaches a number to every point.

- **Congruent segments** are two segments with the same length.
- The **midpoint** of a segment is the point that divides it into two congruent segments.

A midpoint — or any line, ray, or segment passing through a midpoint — is said to **bisect** the segment.

Keep the vocabulary straight: *equal* applies to the numbers (lengths), while *congruent* applies to the figures (segments). Two segments are congruent when their lengths are equal.
