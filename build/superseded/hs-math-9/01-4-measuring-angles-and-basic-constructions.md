# 1.4 Measuring Angles and Basic Constructions

This lesson covers angles and the classical constructions — figures drawn with only a compass and straightedge. Constructions matter beyond the drawing itself: each one is a small proof, showing that a figure can be produced exactly rather than measured approximately.

## Angles

An **angle** is formed by **two rays with the same endpoint**. That shared endpoint is the vertex, and the rays are the sides.

**Congruent angles** are angles with the same measure.

An **angle bisector** is a ray that divides an angle into **two congruent coplanar angles**.

## Perpendicular lines and bisectors

**Perpendicular lines** are two lines that intersect to form **right angles**.

A **perpendicular bisector** is a line, segment, or ray that is perpendicular to a segment **at its midpoint**, thereby bisecting it.

The definition carries two conditions at once — perpendicular *and* through the midpoint — and both are needed. A line can cross a segment at its midpoint without being perpendicular, or perpendicular without passing through the midpoint; neither is a perpendicular bisector.

## The tools

- A **compass** is a geometric tool used to draw circles and parts of circles called **arcs**.
- A **straightedge** is a ruler **with no markings** on it.

The straightedge is unmarked deliberately. A construction may not measure lengths; it may only transfer them with the compass. This restriction is what makes a construction a proof rather than a drawing.

## Constructing a congruent segment

To construct segment $CD$ congruent to a given segment $AB$:

1. Draw a ray with endpoint $C$.
2. Open the compass to the length of $AB$.
3. With the same compass setting, put the compass point on $C$ and draw an arc that intersects the ray. Label the intersection $D$.

The compass carries the length across without ever measuring it.

## Constructing a congruent angle

To construct an angle at $S$ congruent to a given angle $A$:

1. Draw a ray with endpoint $S$.
2. With the compass point on $A$, draw an arc intersecting both sides of angle $A$. Label the intersections $B$ and $C$.
3. With the same compass setting, put the compass point on $S$, draw an arc, and label its intersection with the ray as $R$.
4. Open the compass to the distance from $B$ to $C$. Keeping that setting, put the compass point on $R$ and draw an arc to locate point $T$.
5. Draw ray $ST$.

The construction works by copying the *chord* $BC$ across two arcs of equal radius, which forces the angle to be the same.

## Constructing a perpendicular bisector

To construct the perpendicular bisector $XY$ of segment $AB$:

1. Put the compass point on $A$ and draw a long arc, making sure the opening is **greater than $\tfrac{1}{2}AB$**.
2. With the same compass setting, put the compass point on $B$ and draw another long arc. Label the two points where the arcs intersect $X$ and $Y$.
3. Draw $XY$. The point where it crosses $AB$ is the **midpoint**, and the angles formed are right angles.

The condition in step 1 is not a detail. If the opening is less than half of $AB$, the arcs never meet and there is nothing to join.

## Constructing an angle bisector

To construct the bisector $AX$ of a given angle $A$:

1. Put the compass point on vertex $A$ and draw an arc intersecting both sides. Label the intersections $B$ and $C$.
2. Put the compass point on $C$ and draw an arc. With the same compass setting, draw an arc from $B$, making sure the arcs intersect.
3. Draw ray $AX$ through that intersection.

Both bisector constructions share a method: find a point equidistant from two others, and join it. That is also why the perpendicular bisector construction produces a line every point of which is equidistant from $A$ and $B$ — a property used repeatedly in later work on triangles.
